What the agent does, and what bounds it

These run on a Circle wallet the app provisions for you. You sign in with a passkey; the server signs the transaction; you pay no gas. Every one is capped, and the caps are enforced before anything is signed — not reported afterwards.

Send USDC

To any address on Arc.

Expect: refused above a per-transaction cap, and the error names the exact limit. Irreversible once it confirms — there is no allowlist behind it.

Swap USDC ↔ EURC

On Arc, through the agent wallet.

Expect: the cap is on the USDC-equivalent, not the number you typed, because EURC is not a dollar. The review states a floor you are guaranteed to receive.

Bridge to another chain

USDC off Arc via CCTP.

Expect: capped, and a live fee is taken from the amount. The fee is exact at execution; the arrival is an estimate — the delivered figure appears only once the destination chain has been read.

Recurring swap (DCA)

A schedule that runs while you are away.

Expect: every fill re-checks the per-swap cap and the daily ceiling at execution time, not at setup. A schedule cannot outrun its limits.

Research a question

A sourced brief, with its hash written on-chain.

Expect: pricing is free and runs first — 0.20–0.40 USDC per job. Nothing is delivered or charged until you approve. An evaluator then judges two things only: whether the brief answers the question, and whether its sources are relevant. Not depth, not correctness. Sources are real — they are the pages actually fetched — but a brief with no sources can still ship.

Plan an action

Describe one; the agent proposes.

Expect: a proposal, not an execution. Nothing moves until you approve it separately. The research fee buys the analysis, not the action.

Unified balance

One USDC balance across chains, via Circle Gateway.

Expect: deposits and spends are capped like everything else, and draw on the same daily ceiling.

Four agents, each stoppable

Researcher, Second opinion, Executor, Vault.

Expect: two of them move funds and say so on the card. You can pause any one, or all of them, instantly. One daily ceiling is shared across every agent — a research purchase and a bridge draw on the same budget.

How it behaves when something is wrong

A cap refuses before anything is signed Every action is bounded by a per-transaction cap and one daily ceiling shared across all of them. Going over is refused at the server, before a signature exists — and the refusal names the exact limit it hit, at the moment it applies, rather than sending you to a page to look it up.
Bridged amounts are read, not calculated The fee you are shown is the fee that is charged: the quote is sealed to your wallet, the destination and the amount, and the bridge is refused rather than re-priced if it no longer matches. What arrives is a separate question — the confirmation shows the arrival as an estimate, then advances to a measured figure only after the destination chain has been read. The interface never infers it.
The swap floor is in the calldata, and price-checked The minimum you receive is inside the transaction you sign and enforced by the contract, not promised by the page. It is also valued against an independent reference rate before you sign — and a floor sitting far above mid-market is flagged as anomalous rather than favourable, because it means the two prices disagree. If the check cannot be made, it says so instead of staying quiet.
Refusals appear beside the actions The activity list shows what the agents spent and what a guard stopped. "It tried, and the cap stopped it" is the part worth seeing, so it is not filtered out.

Three things the agent cannot do

Send, bridge and swap also exist in a second form, signed by you in MetaMask. The agent has no part in them — which is exactly why the caps above do not apply.

You hold the key

MetaMask signs; the server does not.

Expect: no spending cap of any kind, because caps bound what the agent may move unattended and nothing here is unattended. You pay the gas. You need MetaMask specifically — a passkey cannot sign these.

Read before you sign

The review is built from the transaction, not the quote.

Expect: the beneficiary is decoded from the transaction bytes and shown in full, and the page says plainly whether it is your wallet or not. If the transaction cannot be read, it refuses to summarise it rather than guessing.

Due diligence answers for Arc Testnet only. Base and Base Sepolia are recognised and deliberately refused: the report says every Arc provider syncs from one permissioned validator set, and that sentence is false elsewhere. A service should not answer for a chain whose assumptions it does not hold. It has its own OpenAPI description, and an on-chain identity you can check without asking us: ownerOf(851891) and tokenURI(851891) on the ERC-8004 registry at 0x8004A818BFB912233c491871b3d84c89A494BD9e resolve to the service owner and to a content-addressed document whose hash you can verify yourself. Its own identity endpoint tells you to trust that endpoint last and read the chain instead — the service flags trusted-mutable surfaces in other people’s contracts, and does not exempt itself.
