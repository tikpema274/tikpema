An agent that moves money under caps you can read

Tikpema runs an AI agent on a wallet of your own. It can send, swap, bridge, research and settle on-chain jobs — each action bounded by a per-transaction cap and one shared daily ceiling, each refusal named rather than silent. The point isn't that an agent can move money. It's that you can see exactly what would stop it.

Moving stablecoins is the easy part — Arc settles it in under a second, in USDC, with no second gas token. The hard part is letting software do it on your behalf. Tikpema is the layer that makes that safe to watch: every agent action is capped before it is signed, every refusal is named, and the three operations that carry no cap are the ones you sign yourself.

The agent plane

The agent acts — under caps

Runs on a Circle wallet the app provisions for you. You sign in with a passkey; the server signs the transaction; you pay no gas. Every action is bounded, and the caps are enforced before anything is signed — not reported afterwards.

Your plane

You act — no cap at all

Send, bridge and swap also exist in a second form, signed by you in MetaMask. The agent has no part in them, which is exactly why the caps do not apply — and you pay the gas. You sign those yourself.

The agent plane

What the agent does, and what bounds it

Each of these runs under a per-transaction cap and one daily ceiling shared across every agent. The caps are enforced at the server before a signature exists — and when one refuses, it names the exact limit at the moment it applies.

Send USDC

To any address on Arc.

Expect: refused above a per-transaction cap, and the error names the exact limit. Irreversible once it confirms — there is no allowlist behind it.

Swap USDC ↔ EURC

On Arc, through the agent wallet.

Expect: the cap is on the USDC-equivalent, not the number you typed, because EURC is not a dollar. The review states a floor you are guaranteed to receive, and flags a floor sitting far above mid-market as anomalous, not favourable.

Bridge to another chain

USDC off Arc via CCTP.

Expect: capped, and a live fee is taken from the amount. The fee is exact at execution; the arrival is an estimate — the delivered figure appears only once the destination chain has been read.

Recurring swap (DCA)

A schedule that runs while you are away.

Expect: every fill re-checks the per-swap cap and the daily ceiling at execution time, not at setup. A schedule cannot outrun its limits.

Unified balance

One USDC balance across Arc and Base, via Circle Gateway.

Expect: deposits and spends are capped like everything else and draw on the same daily ceiling. Getting funds out runs through us — that is stated at the deposit itself, not buried.

Plan an action

Describe one in words; the agent proposes.

Expect: a proposal, not an execution. Nothing moves until you approve it separately, and the proposed step carries the same caps as any other.

Four agents, each stoppable

Researcher · Second opinion · Executor · Vault.

Expect: two of them move funds and say so on the card. You can pause any one, or all of them, instantly. One daily ceiling is shared across every agent — a research purchase and a bridge draw on the same budget.

A vault it can inspect

Deposit into an allowlisted ERC-4626 vault — after it reads the vault.

Expect: before a deposit, the agent reports the vault's owner powers, withdraw mechanics and whether it is funded — as plain disclosure, framed as what to know, never as an accusation. The deposit is capped and gated on your acknowledgement.

How it behaves when something is wrong

A cap refuses before anything is signed Every action is bounded by a per-transaction cap and one daily ceiling shared across all of them. Going over is refused at the server, before a signature exists — and the refusal names the exact limit it hit, at the moment it applies, rather than sending you to a page to look it up.
Bridged amounts are read, not calculated The fee you are shown is the fee that is charged: the quote is sealed to your wallet, the destination and the amount, and the bridge is refused rather than re-priced if it no longer matches. What arrives is a separate question — the confirmation shows arrival as an estimate, then advances to a measured figure only after the destination chain has been read. The interface never infers it.
The swap floor is in the calldata, and price-checked The minimum you receive is inside the transaction you sign and enforced by the contract, not promised by the page. It is also valued against an independent reference rate before you sign — and a floor sitting far above mid-market is flagged as anomalous rather than favourable, because it means the two prices disagree. If the check cannot be made, it says so instead of staying quiet.
Refusals appear beside the actions The activity list shows what the agents spent and what a guard stopped. "It tried, and the cap stopped it" is the part worth seeing, so it is not filtered out.
On-chain jobs · ERC-8183

Research it can settle on-chain

Ask the agent a research question and it runs as a real on-chain job: funded into escrow, answered as a sourced brief, judged, and settled — or refunded. Today this is a personal loop — your own agent funds its own research budget, so the same wallet is client, provider and evaluator — but the escrow, the state machine and the USDC settlement are the real ERC-8183 lifecycle, not a mock.
01

Priced first

The agent prices the job at 0.20–0.40 USDC, or declines it if it is advice rather than research. Nothing is charged until you approve.

02

Funded into escrow

On approval the budget is funded into the job's on-chain escrow. The work runs against that budget.

03

Hash submitted

The brief's keccak256 hash is submitted on-chain; the bytes themselves are held off-chain. The chain proves integrity, not availability.

04

Judged, then settled

An evaluator returns a verdict, which settles the escrow to the provider or refunds it.

The evaluator is a second, tools-less model call, and it judges exactly two things: whether the brief responsively answers the question, and whether its cited sources are relevant — not depth, correctness or completeness. Sources are the pages actually fetched, but a brief with no sources can still ship: citation enforcement is off in this environment, and the page says so rather than promising "cited every time". A refund can be a quality verdict from the judge, or an earlier force-reject that never reached it — those are different, and the app labels which.
Your plane

Three things the agent cannot do

Send, bridge and swap also exist in a second form, signed by you in MetaMask. The agent has no part in them — which is why the caps above do not apply, and why you sign those yourself.

You hold the key

MetaMask signs; the server does not.

Expect: no spending cap of any kind, because caps bound what the agent may move unattended and nothing here is unattended. You pay the gas. You need MetaMask specifically — a passkey cannot sign these.

Read before you sign

The review is built from the transaction, not the quote.

Expect: the beneficiary is decoded from the transaction bytes and shown in full, and the page says plainly whether it is your wallet or not. If the transaction cannot be read, it refuses to summarise it rather than guessing.
Paid services · x402

What it sells

Base and Base Sepolia are recognised and deliberately refused: the report says every Arc provider syncs from one permissioned validator set, and that sentence is false elsewhere — a service should not answer for a chain whose assumptions it does not hold. It carries its own OpenAPI description and an on-chain identity you can check without asking us: ownerOf(851891) and tokenURI(851891) on the ERC-8004 registry at 0x8004A818BFB912233c491871b3d84c89A494BD9e resolve to the service owner and to a content-addressed document whose hash you can verify yourself. Its own identity endpoint tells you to trust that endpoint last and read the chain instead — it flags trusted-mutable surfaces in other people's contracts, and does not exempt itself.
